Texas SB 840

July 2026 · Andres Gamez

Over the past year and a half I worked with the TCU Center for Urban Studies on a parcel-level analysis of Texas Senate Bill 840, the 2025 law that lets qualifying multifamily and mixed-use housing be built on land already zoned for commercial, office, retail, warehouse, and mixed-use activity in large Texas cities. The question we set out to answer was simple to state and hard to measure: how much room for housing does SB 840 actually open up inside the land Texas cities have already urbanized?

Legal eligibility is not the same as redevelopment. A parcel can be legally eligible for multifamily housing and still be a poor candidate for near-term conversion because of its owner, tenants, buildings, leases, infrastructure, or market conditions. The work translates SB 840's legal language into a parcel geography across 18 zoned Texas cities, applies the statutory exclusions, and then compares conversion likelihood against financial upside to find where the two signals agree.

I have published the findings as a three-part public research-note series with interactive maps and charts. It is written for planners, policymakers, journalists, and anyone curious about the effects of SB 840 without reading the full technical paper. The series is a coauthored work with Sean M. Crotty and Kyle Walker.

Open the SB 840 research note series →

The series is organized into three posts:

  • Post 1 — What Could SB 840 Actually Change? Housing and fiscal context, the 2025 housing package, and how we measured the parcel geography.
  • Post 2 — Where Does SB 840 Actually Apply? Turning statewide statute into local zoning and parcel geography.
  • Post 3 — What Is Actually Likely To Happen Under SB 840? Comparing conversion likelihood and financial upside to find where they align.

The series also includes interactive parcel effect maps and analysis result maps for each of the 18 study cities. You can explore them directly from the series landing page.
